The story / In brief

Giving online used to mean filling in a long form. As founding designer, I helped Fundraise Up replace it with a checkout built around one decision at a time.

The established pattern

The industry standard: a donation that feels like paperwork.

When Fundraise Up started, the accepted way to donate online was one long page: donation details, personal information, billing address, payment and extra options, all at once. Donors had to face every field before making their first real decision — how much to give.

Every extra field is another reason to hesitate, and on a phone the form turns into an endless scroll.

Fundraise Up set out to do the opposite.

01 / THE IDEA

One decision at a time

Instead of sending donors to a separate page full of fields, the checkout opens as a compact widget on the nonprofit’s own site. It starts with the only question that matters first — how much, and how often — and asks for payment details only after that choice is made.

As the founding designer, I turned that idea into a working donation experience. In a small team, the work moved between interface detail, product priorities and communication without a large structure around it.

02 / THE FEEL

Keep the donor moving

A short form still needs momentum. I used gamification and motion to guide each step: a clear choice between one-time and monthly giving, suggested amounts that adapt to that choice and small moments of positive feedback. This increased conversion into monthly payments and made recurring giving easy to understand from the start.

The donation button carried the experience from processing to a clear success state. Immediate animated feedback reduced the perceived wait and removed the feeling of a long loading screen while the payment was being processed.

03 / THE RESULT

Less paperwork, more giving

The overall donation conversion increased 2.8×, with donor satisfaction at around 98% CSAT.

Clarity became the competitive advantage. The compact checkout helped a small company compete with much larger, established platforms whose donation journeys still relied on long forms.

04 / THE OTHER SIDE

Design for the nonprofit, too

A checkout is only half of the product. I also worked on the surfaces nonprofits use every day: the campaign overview brought donations, recurring gifts and supporters into one clear operating view.

05 / EARLY-STAGE DESIGN

Explain it before it exists

Founding design included early pitch and marketing materials as well as the interface itself. That breadth taught me how design can help a company explain what it is building before its product and team are fully formed.
